<div style="display: flex; align-items: flex-start;">
    <div style="flex: 1;">
        I use abstract mathematical methods to rigorously prove the existence and uniqueness of specialized solutions to the
        <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_equations">Navier-Stokes equations</a>, which are fundamental equations used to describe
        fluid flow, and study their properties. I extensively use tools from Nonlinear Partial Differential Equations,
        Functional Analysis, Harmonic/Fourier Analysis, and Sobolev space theory, to study these equations. The
        Navier-Stokes equations are widely used in applications, from weather modeling to aircraft design,
        however they are notoriously complex and many fundamental questions related to these equations remain
        unsolved (including the famous <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_existence_and_smoothness">million dollar Millennium problem</a>). The rigorous mathematical study of
        these equations ensures the models used by Engineers and Scientists in applications are robust and
        possess a solid theoretical underpinning.
    </div>

My research specifically addresses the existence and uniqueness
of traveling wave solutions under various physical conditions, and my work verifies that the Navier-Stokes
equations can accurately capture the physics in physically relevant situations, thereby proving the
reliability of these fundamental models in more complex physical scenarios.

<img src="/assets/img/publication_preview/fluiddomain.png" alt="Fluid Domain Preview" style="display: block; margin: auto; max-width: 50%; height: auto;">
<p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic;">Figure 1: A toy picture of an evolving viscous surface wave with a free upper surface \( \Sigma(t) \) and a fixed lower surface \( \Sigma_0 \) in 2D.</p>

For my <a href="https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/thesis/Traveling_wave_solutions_to_the_free_boundary_incompressible_Navier-Stokes_equations/24053892?file=42184224">Ph.D. thesis</a>, I focused on the analysis of viscous surface waves which are modeled via
the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, with the presence of an upper <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_boundary_problem">free boundary</a> and
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_tension">surface tension</a>. A physical example of this is the water waves generated by wind traveling through a
river, where the river bottom is a fixed rigid surface and the evolving water surface a free boundary.

<img src="/assets/img/publication_preview/incline.png" alt="Incline Preview" style="display: block; margin: auto; max-width: 50%; height: auto;">
<p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic;">Figure 2: A toy picture of an evolving viscous wave traveling down an inclined surface.</p>

In joint work with my advisor <a href="https://www.math.cmu.edu/~iantice/">Ian Tice</a>, we were able to demonstrate that traveling wave
solutions are generic with the presence of an external bulk force and/or applied surface stress under a
variety of physical considerations. Traveling waves are physically important as they are empirically
observed daily throughout the physical world and mathematically interesting as they arise naturally
in a variety of mathematical models, though rigorously demonstrating their existence is
mathematically challenging as the underlying models are notoriously complex. While the
construction in the inviscid case (for non-viscous fluids and without free boundary) has been known
for a long time, the rigorous construction for the viscous free boundary problem was only recently
established by <a href="https://giovannileoni.weebly.com/">Giovanni Leoni</a> and <a href="https://www.math.cmu.edu/~iantice/">Ian Tice</a> in <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cpa.22084">this paper</a> in 2022. We were able to improve upon the
functional analytic framework developed by Leoni and Tice to prove that traveling wave solutions
remain generic under periodization and inclination of the fluid domain, and also under replacing the
standard <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-slip_condition">no-slip boundary conditions</a> at the fluid bottom with the so-called Navier-slip conditions.
